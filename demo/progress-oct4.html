From an audience question to three reviewed drafts

Verified on 4 October 2026. This page presents development evidence, not approved medical campaign content.

What is new

Earlier drafts used full reference documents. Today's completed run used hybrid retrieval to select passages, a separate model check to keep the topic in scope, and the Streamlit writer to generate all three formats.

  1. One real audience comment prompted the question: how long will scleral lenses last?
  2. Nine source summaries yielded eleven passages. Hybrid search returned six candidates.
  3. One proposed topic passed the scope check; seven factual statements were reviewed against their citations.
  4. Gemini generated LinkedIn, a short blog and Google Business Profile drafts.
  5. Codex reviewed and corrected unsupported wording before preparing this saved sample.

What review caught

The topic described the question as frequent despite citing one comment. The blog added a management claim and inferred that handling could extend lens life. These were corrected in the displayed copies. Original Gemini outputs and the correction log are retained.

The reviewed drafts contain 143, 167 and 144 words respectively. The blog remains short because the available evidence does not justify padding it.

Clinical review remains pending. Codex editorial review does not establish medical correctness. The app did not automatically perform these editorial corrections.

Verification

57 automated tests passed. Actual local-model retrieval found the expected document for 9/9 original answerable questions and 6/6 additional questions, compared with 7/9 and 5/6 using BM25. These are small hand-labelled tests, including synthetic software documents, not a general accuracy estimate.

The three new live drafts passed reference, CTA and punctuation checks. Their wording was reviewed separately. No campaign content was posted to social platforms.
