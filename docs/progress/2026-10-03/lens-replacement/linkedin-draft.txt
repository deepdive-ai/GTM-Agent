Scleral Lens Replacement: What to Expect

Scleral lenses generally have an expected lifespan of one to two years, though some lenses may last longer. However, there is no fixed replacement deadline for every patient, as the expected lifespan of an individual's scleral lenses can vary based on factors such as the lens brand, material, and personal circumstances.

Several reasons may lead to a scleral lens needing replacement. These include the accumulation of deposits, damage like surface cracking or breakage, or changes in the lens fit or prescription power. The surfaces of gas-permeable scleral lenses can deteriorate due to repeated wearing and handling, though careful handling can help preserve the lens surface. Re-polishing a lens is not always a viable option.

If a scleral lens needs replacement, it may not be an identical copy. The existing design might be duplicated, adjusted, or a new fitting may be required. It is recommended to inform an eye-care provider about any changes in lens fit or vision, lenses that repeatedly shift, or lenses that become difficult to remove.
