Understanding the Lifespan of Scleral Lenses for Keratoconus

While Cleveland Clinic states that scleral lenses generally have an expected lifespan of one to two years, some may last longer. This is general information and not a fixed replacement deadline for every patient.

The actual lifespan of an individual's scleral lenses can vary. Factors such as the lens brand, material, and personal circumstances all play a role in how long a lens might last.

Several reasons might lead to a scleral lens needing replacement. These can include the accumulation of deposits on the lens, damage such as surface cracking or breakage, or changes in the lens fit or prescription power. The surfaces of gas-permeable scleral lenses can also deteriorate over time due to repeated wearing and handling.

Careful handling of scleral lenses can help preserve their surface. It is advisable to inform an eye-care provider if you notice changes in lens fit or vision, if your lenses repeatedly shift out of place, or if they become difficult to remove.
